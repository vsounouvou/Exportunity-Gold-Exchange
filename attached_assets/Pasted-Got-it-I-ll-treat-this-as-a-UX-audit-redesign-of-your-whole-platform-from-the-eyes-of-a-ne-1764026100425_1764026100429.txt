## 1️⃣ From the eyes of a new founder: what’s confusing today?

Persona:
**“I’m a founder. I’ve just signed up. I want a ready-made AI team that actually does work for my company.”**

From the screenshot + what you told me:

* Top nav: `Home | Agents & Clones | Workspace | Management`
* Inside Agents & Clones: tabs `Your Team | Discover | Expert Clones | Hierarchy`
* Metrics: “14 Total Agents, 14 Active Agents, 0 Expert Clones, 1 Departments”
* Company switcher: “Default Company”

**Main cognitive problems for a first-time user:**

1. **Too many concepts at once**
   “Agents”, “Clones”, “Expert Clones”, “Hierarchy”, “Workspace”, “Management”, “Departments”… but I still don’t know:

   * *What should I do first?*
   * *How do these pieces work together?*

2. **“Expert Clones” vs “Your Team” vs “Discover” is ambiguous**

   * Am I hiring? configuring? browsing a marketplace? It’s not obvious.
   * You already decided clones + marketplace are one thing; the UI must reflect that.

3. **Workspace isn’t clearly about *doing work***
   You mentioned: instead of just “Meetings, Knowledge base”, it should feel like:

   * **Actions**
   * **Tasks**
   * **Things getting done for me by agents**

4. **Onboarding is not guided**

   * There is no visible “Start here” to:

     1. Confirm my company
     2. Meet my AI team
     3. Give them their first mission
     4. See a result

5. **Homepage likely doesn’t tell a story**
   It needs to say in one glance:

   > “You now have a full AI team. Here’s what they’re doing for your company today.”

## 2️⃣ New Information Architecture & Pages (UX Redesign)

### A. Top Navigation (simplified)

**Proposed top bar:**

1. **Home** – Overview & onboarding
2. **AI Team** (replaces “Agents & Clones”)
3. **Work** (replaces “Workspace”)
4. **Company** (replaces “Management”)
5. Company switcher: `Default Company ▼` (top-left or top-right)

This already tells a story:

> *Home → AI Team you have → Work they’re doing → Company settings*

---

### B. Home: “Your AI Company Control Room”

**Goal:** in 10 seconds, user understands what they have and what to do.

**Hero section:**

* Title: **“Your AI team is ready to work.”**
* Subtitle: **“12 specialists already assigned to your company. Give them a mission.”**
* Primary CTA: **“Give them your first mission”** → opens a short 3-step wizard under *Work*.
* Secondary CTA: **“Review your AI team”** → goes to *AI Team > Your Team*.

**Below hero – 3 columns:**

1. **AI Team snapshot**

   * “12 Core Agents”
   * Small chips: `Operations`, `Finance`, `Strategy`, `Support`, `Automation`…
   * Link: **“View full team”**

2. **Work in progress**

   * “Today: 0 tasks running, 0 completed”
   * Once active: list last 3 tasks.

3. **Company setup**

   * Checkmarks:

     * ✅ Company created
     * ⬜ Connect payment gateway
     * ⬜ Invite human teammates (optional)

Each item is clickable to complete.

### C. AI Team (ex “Agents & Clones”)

Tabs redesigned + renamed for clarity:

* **Your Team** – agents assigned to this company
* **Marketplace** – discover new expert clones & templates
* **Hierarchy** – org chart view, per company

You already decided **expert clones + personal clones = one concept**. So:

#### 1. Your Team

Grid or list of the 12 core roles:

* Card example:

  * Title: **Head of Operations**
  * Short line: “Turns strategy into repeatable workflows”
  * Badges: `Execution`, `Processes`
  * Buttons:

    * **“Ask to design a workflow”**
    * **“View role details”**

On top of the page:

* Short text:

  > “This is your default AI team. Each role is already configured for **Default Company**. You can edit or deactivate them any time.”

#### 2. Marketplace

This replaces “Discover” + “Expert Clones” confusion:

* Filters:

  * `Role type` (Ops, Finance, Sales, Support, Product…)
  * `Industry presets` (optional, later)
* Card example:

  * Title: “Sales Pipeline Coach”
  * Use case: “Helps you design and improve your sales funnel.”
  * Button: **“Add to my AI team”**

When user adds one, it appears under **Your Team** for that company.

#### 3. Hierarchy

Simple org chart, **per company**:

* Top: **Chief Orchestrator / Chief of Staff**
* Below: **Ops / Finance / Strategy / Innovation**
* Below them: rest of agents.

Interactions:

* Click an agent node → view their detail; toggle active/inactive for this company.
* Option to switch view: `Roles view` / `Agent view`.

### D. Work (new Workspace)

**Core idea:** this is where the user sees and controls **what’s being done**.

Tabs inside Work:

1. **Actions** (default)
2. **Projects**
3. **Meetings**
4. **Knowledge**

#### 1. Actions (default)

This is the **main “to-do / done” screen**.

* Table or cards listing:

  * Action title (“Design onboarding workflow”, “Analyze last week’s payments”)
  * Status: `Queued`, `In progress`, `Done`, `Needs Review`
  * Owner: `Head of Operations`, `Data & Insights Analyst`, etc.
  * Company: `Default Company` (useful later when multi-company)

Top of page:

* CTA: **“Create a new mission”**

  * Wizard:

    1. What do you want to achieve? (short text or pick template)
    2. Which area? (Ops / Finance / Strategy / Support / etc.)
    3. Output type? (Plan, Workflow, Analysis, Email, Document)

  Orchestrator then:

  * Assigns to appropriate agent,
  * Creates 1–3 sub-actions automatically.

#### 2. Projects

Clusters of actions:

* Each project has:

  * Name, description
  * Owner agent
  * Progress bar (# tasks completed / total)
* Example projects:

  * “Set up payment gateway and reporting”
  * “Design customer support flow”
  * “Launch new product page”

#### 3. Meetings

**Per company**, as you required.

List:

* Upcoming meetings
* Past meetings
* Each has:

  * Topic
  * Participants (agents + humans)
  * Summary & action items (linked back to **Actions**)

Create Meeting flow:

* “Why are you meeting?” (goal)
* Suggest participants: Orchestrator proposes which agents are relevant.
* After meeting: Coordination Manager auto-generates summary + tasks.

#### 4. Knowledge

Not a dead “knowledge base”; a **working memory**:

* Split into:

  * `Company docs`
  * `Playbooks & SOPs` (created by Knowledge Curator)
  * `Agent briefs` (what each agent learned for this company)

From here:

* User can upload docs → they’re tagged by company + suggested agents.
* Agents show “Last used” for each doc.

## 3️⃣ Micro-UX: details that make it feel seamless

### A. First-time onboarding flow (critical)

**Step 1: Welcome**

* Message:

  > “You now have a full AI team. Let’s connect them to your company in 2 minutes.”

* Choices:

  * **“I’m just exploring”** (demo mode, pre-populated example data)
  * **“I want to use this for my real company”**

**Step 2: Company basics**

* Ask:

  * Company name
  * Industry (optional)
  * Main goal for the next 30 days (free text)

Use that goal to auto-create:

* 2–3 **starter projects** in Work.
* Personalized text on Home.

**Step 3: Meet your AI team**

* Show a **horizontal carousel** of the 12 roles:

  * Role title + 1-line explanation + example question.

At end:

* CTA: **“Give them your first mission”** → go to Work → “Create mission” with a pre-filled suggestion based on user’s goal.

---

### B. Language / wording cleanup

* “Agents & Clones” → **AI Team**
* “Expert Clones” / “Discover” → **Marketplace**
* “Workspace” → **Work**
* “Management” → **Company**
* “Departments” → **Teams** (if you keep it; or hide until needed)

Try to keep **one concept = one word**:

* Team → roles
* Work → tasks/actions
* Meetings → specific, time-bound events
* Knowledge → docs & playbooks
* Company → settings & integrations

---

### C. Feedback & state

* Every action triggered by user should show:

  * **“Which agent is now working on it”**
  * A **visible status chip** in Actions.
* If something is not yet implemented technically:

  * Use copy like: “Coming soon” but hide it by default; don’t show dead buttons.

---

### D. Safe defaults

On first login, automatically:

* Assign the 12 core roles to **Default Company**.
* Create **one demo project** with 2 completed actions so the dashboard is not empty:

  * “Example: Analyze last week’s performance”
  * “Example: Draft a welcome email to new customers”

Let users delete or turn off demo content later.
