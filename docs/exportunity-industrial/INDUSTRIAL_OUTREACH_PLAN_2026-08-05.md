# Exportunity Industrial Outreach Plan

Prepared: 2026-08-05

Status: planning only. No email, WhatsApp message, call, social post, account creation or advertising spend has been authorized or executed by this plan.

## Commercial Objective

Exportunity Machinery is a demand-driven industrial operating system. The first commercial objective is not mass marketing. It is to validate recurring industrial demand, secure inventory and supplier relationships, and convert qualified requirements into paid marketplace, sourcing, scan-to-manufacture, maintenance and logistics work.

The first 90 days follow the business-plan sequence:

1. Build a verified industrial database.
2. Interview industrial buyers and capture recurring requirements.
3. Onboard importers, distributors and machinery dealers with existing stock.
4. Qualify fabrication, machining, maintenance and engineering suppliers.
5. Secure letters of intent for scan-to-manufacture demand.
6. Publish only verified products, factories and capabilities.

## Priority Segments

### A. Anchor industrial buyers

Factories in cotton, textile, agro-processing, cement, beverages, packaging, construction materials and pharmaceuticals.

Primary conversation:

- Which imported spare parts repeatedly cause downtime?
- Which parts have long lead times or high freight cost?
- Which SKUs are predictable enough to stock locally?
- Which worn or unavailable parts can Exportunity scan, digitize or reverse engineer?

### B. Inventory and distribution partners

Importers, distributors, industrial-equipment dealers and machinery after-sales companies.

Primary conversation:

- Exportunity can digitize and expose existing inventory without taking the partner's clients.
- Partners keep commercial ownership and approve publication.
- Exportunity supplies qualified demand, structured RFQs and technical context.

### C. Technical suppliers

Machinists, welders, CNC operators, fabricators, maintenance firms, foundries and engineering teams.

Primary conversation:

- Capabilities, equipment, tolerances, materials and quality process
- Capacity, lead time and service area
- NDA and drawing-handling readiness
- Trial-job and supplier-scorecard process

### D. Logistics and institutions

Port operators, freight companies, terminals, APIEx, GDIZ/SIPI and CIPB.

Primary conversation:

- Import/export routing and industrial cargo visibility
- Factory introductions and roundtables
- Supplier verification and zone-based delivery
- Export-readiness support

## Approval-Gated Workflow

1. Research Agent proposes a source-backed record.
2. Industrial Qualification Agent verifies the entity and records an opportunity hypothesis.
3. Account Manager identifies the correct role and public business channel.
4. Outreach Agent prepares a company-specific French or English draft.
5. Compliance Agent checks identity, lawful basis, suppression list, quiet hours and channel rules.
6. A human administrator reviews and approves the exact recipient, sender, message and channel.
7. Communications Agent sends one approved message and writes the audit record.
8. Reply Router links the response to the correct lead, meeting, task and next action.
9. Follow-up is created only when permitted; no uncontrolled sequence runs in the background.

## 90-Day Program

### Days 1-15: evidence and interviews

- Verify the 38 importable prospects in the initial Benin cohort.
- Obtain the current complete APIEx/GDIZ company list rather than inferring the 11 names not exposed on the public page.
- Identify 20 high-fit plant, maintenance, procurement or operations contacts.
- Prepare one-page buyer interview and inventory-partner interview scripts.
- Configure the official Exportunity sender domain and inbox; test deliverability internally before contacting any prospect.

Exit gate: 20 verified records, 10 approved contact drafts, zero messages sent without approval.

### Days 16-30: controlled founder-led outreach

- Run a first batch of no more than 10 individually approved messages.
- Prioritize warm introductions through APIEx, GDIZ/SIPI and CIPB.
- Book discovery meetings around downtime, imported spares and inventory visibility.
- Record each answer as structured requirements, not free-text notes only.

Exit gate: delivery/reply data reviewed, opt-outs reconciled, message adjusted before any next batch.

### Days 31-60: commercial validation

- Target 20 importer/distributor meetings.
- Secure at least 10 letters of intent for recurring requirements or inventory visibility.
- Build the first verified 100-SKU industrial catalogue from partner-approved records.
- Qualify technical suppliers against capability, quality, NDA and lead-time criteria.
- Create the first scan-to-manufacture pilot dossiers.

Exit gate: every catalogue item has an owner, evidence, availability statement and review date.

### Days 61-90: conversion and repeatability

- Convert qualified requirements into RFQs, quotes and paid orders.
- Target 50 paid orders only after supply, pricing and delivery controls are proven.
- Publish case studies only with written customer permission.
- Review margins separately for marketplace commission, sourcing, engineering, warehousing and maintenance.
- Build a repeatable account plan for the highest-value factory segments.

Paid promotion should begin only after a working conversion path exists. The first budget should promote a proven industrial offer to a narrow job-title and geography, not generic brand awareness. Account creation, ad funding and publication on Facebook, TikTok or other platforms require the user's explicit approval and authenticated account access at execution time.
